# YPL League Almanac

**리그 연감: 리그가 매 시즌 펴내는 공식 기록집. 면 없이 종이 위에 글자와 줄로 짓고, 큰 숫자는 가늘게 세운다.**

The site is the league's almanac. The navy ground is the page. Nothing stands on a box: an entry is set on the page in type and closed by a hairline, and a section opens with a thick rule. Emphasis is size, weight and the thick rule: the one decided, current or chosen thing in a group is set lit over a 2px rule, the rest dim or muted. Numbers the page announces are large figures set thin in Albert Sans, the larger the thinner. The only colour beside navy is an amber mark for what is live, and the types' own colours. Nothing flies; things arrive.

Two things never change: the golden ratio behind every measure, and the navy `#081C3D`.

## Philosophy

YPL is the league's official record, read on a phone at the venue between matches. So the site reads like a well-set almanac, not like a club homepage and not like a machine.

- **It records, it does not decorate.** Every figure on a screen is read from data. Where there is no fact the page stays empty; it never fills the space with an illustration or a claim.
- **What is happening now comes first,** then the record, then the archive.
- **Emphasis is size, weight and the rule, not colour.** One hue, navy. The decided thing is lit, larger and over a thick rule; the rest is dim or muted. Amber marks what is live and nothing else.
- **Type and rules, not boxes.** No surface, no radius, no shadow, no blur, no glow. What separates is space and a hairline; what opens a section is a 2px rule.
- **The big number is the voice.** A figure the page announces (a rank, a generation, the days to an event, a score) is set large and thin, as an almanac sets its standing figures. Everything read in a line is type.
- **It arrives; it does not fly.** Things come on by rising the last 12 into place as they appear, and nothing that stands moves again.
- **One ratio decides.** Space, type, heights, splits and time all come from phi.
- **Clean, simple, refined.** When a screen feels busy the fix is to take away, never to add a layer (the owner, 2026-11-24: "깔끔하고 심플하고 세련되고 숫자를 크게 쓰는").
- **Made by people.** Nothing on the site may read as made by a machine (Made by people).

## Where the law lives

This file is the law. `src/styles/tokens.css` holds its numbers, `src/components/common/Board.jsx` and `src/styles/board.css` its shared parts (rows, counters, a Pokémon on its floor, fields, choices, tabs, chips, the dock, toasts), and `tests/designLaw.test.mjs` checks every measurable rule on every stylesheet and inline style. A new screen is built from those parts. A change to the law goes here first, then into the code; history is in git, not here.

## Principles

| Principle | Why | So we do not |
|---|---|---|
| Size, weight and the rule are the emphasis | An almanac ranks by setting, not by colour | Colour text to stress it, fill a box behind it, or add a second hue for status |
| Facts replace decoration | Records are why the site exists | Add illustrations, empty badges or typed numbers; every figure is read from data |
| Entries, not boxes | A page of record is set, not framed | Give a thing a panel, a card, a frame, a radius or a shadow |
| Big numbers are thin figures | A standing figure is the almanac's voice | Draw a number as a segment display, a dial or a pip row, or set a reading number (a table cell, a date in a line) as a figure |
| Things arrive, they do not fly | A page is set, it does not perform | Slide pages, bounce cards or move what already stands |
| The venue phone is the measure | It is the main scene | Hide function behind hover, or go under 44 to touch |
| One kind of entry, many forms | A good almanac mixes a table, a standing list, a figure and a portrait | Repeat one row shape down a screen when the content asks for a table or a figure |
| Only this league could say it | Specific facts make a page look made by people | Ship a section, sentence or effect any other site could carry unchanged |

## How the law is written

A law is one of three kinds, and each kind lives in one place.

| Kind | When | Where it lives | Checked by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A number** | a value the eye compares across the site | a token in `tokens.css`, written once in The golden law | `tests/designLaw.test.mjs` |
| **A measure on the screen** | ink to ink, ink to edge, a Pokémon's feet on its floor | The eye, as a distance and a tolerance | the browser at 390 and 1440, never the CSS alone |
| **A principle** | a choice of meaning: what lights, what inverts, which lamp, the voice | words with examples, no figure of its own | review against this file |

A number is written once; other chapters name the token and its job. History goes in git, not here.

## The golden law

**One ratio, phi (1.618).** No measure is picked by eye.

| Measure | Law | Values |
|---|---|---|
| Rules | a hairline closes an entry, a 2px rule opens a section or marks the decided one | 1, 2 |
| Space | 4 times a Fibonacci number | 4, 8, 12, 20, 32, 52, 84 |
| Type | four sizes, each line on the 4 grid | 12/16, 15/20, 20/28, 32/40; reading paragraphs 15/24 |
| Figure | the height of a figure's digits, set in Albert Sans at font-size height / .72; 200 from 84, 300 from 40, 400 under | 32 and up, each height set from the space tokens: 32, 40 and 52 in a line or a counter, 84 and 120 for a block's figure, 136 to 356 for the one figure a page stands on (the podium, the hall's generation, 404) |
| Height | a strip, the touch floor, a row, a two-line row, a counter | 32, 44, 52, 64, 84 |
| Split | 1.618 to 1 | the champion's text and picture (132 to 216), the desktop columns (709 and 439 in 1200), a counter to a row (84 to 52) |
| Time | each verb about 1.618 times the last | 100, 160, 260, 420 |
| Stagger | 100 by 1/phi and 1/phi squared | 62 between displays, 38 between rows |
| Press | 1 minus 1/phi to the 8th | .976 |
| Floor | a quarter of a picture cell, or the line of words under it | 24% |

Outside the law on purpose: the 44 a finger needs; the 1 and 2px lines (the hairline halves to .5 on a 2x screen); the icon grid of 16, 20 and 24; the Wing Y's own proportion; navy `#081C3D`; the content width and breakpoints, which sit where content breaks.

## Hierarchy

**Everything on a screen stands on one of six tiers, and a tier is one look.** The tiers are the page's order of importance; the golden law gives their numbers. A thing takes the look of its tier and nothing else, so the eye ranks a screen without reading it.

| Tier | What stands there | Look | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Figure | what the page announces: the countdown, a clock, a score, a counter, a rank on a podium, a generation, 404 | a thin figure (Fig: 120 or 240 hero, 84, 40, 32) | none: an image role with its value as text |
| 1 Page | the page's name (기록, 공지, 대진표, 배틀); a thing's own title when it opens in the page's place (a notice, a post, a bracket) | the name `.page-title`, 52 on a phone and 84 on desktop, 700, lit; a thing's own title 32/40, 700 | `h1`, one a page |
| 2 Section | a section's or a sheet's title; the one name an entry exists for (a lead notice, the current champion, a placing's winner) | 20/28, 700, lit | `h2` for a section, `h3` for a sheet, none for a name in an entry |
| 3 Row | a row's title, a name in a list, a button's word, a field's value, a tab | 15/20, 700 (titles, buttons) or 600 (fields, tabs) | `h4` only for an entry's own title |
| 4 Reading | a body, an explanation | 15/24, 400 | `p` |
| 5 Label | a label, a grey line, a unit, a hint | 12/16 (15/20 on desktop), 600 a label, 400 a grey line, dim | never a heading: a label over a control is its label |

- **One tier, one look.** No fifth size, no colour, no weight outside the tier's. A tier's size is never borrowed to lift something: emphasis inside a tier is light (lit, then dim, then muted) and, for the one decided, current or chosen thing of a group, the decided look (Emphasis).
- **Two exceptions, both named.** A page led by a figure has its figure as tier 0: on 404 the `h1` is the figure's caption, 20 on a phone and 32 on desktop; on the home the next event's name is the `h1` at 32 over its countdown (draft B). The decided record's one name, the reigning champion on the hall's crown and a bracket's winner on its 우승 line, stands at 32: the record exists for it.
- **When two things of one tier meet,** the page's order ranks them (what is happening now first, then the record, then the archive), and then their light; never a size between the tiers.
- **Actions have three tiers** and only three: the main action (a filled lit key, 52, its words in `--on-lit`, its mark before the word: → when it goes on, ✓ when it commits, + when it creates; one a screen and one a sheet), the second action (a 1px `--lit` outline, 52, or 44 in a row of small actions), the link (an underlined word). A sheet's or dialog's confirm is its main action; a destructive confirm is the main action too, without the ✓ (a ✓ says done; a delete is not a thing done well), its sentence says what it destroys, and the keyboard's focus starts on the way back. Beside a back action the main action stands last and larger, 1.618 to 1.
- **There is one surface, the ground.** An entry stands on it closed by a hairline, a section opens on the 2px rule, `--tint` is the one step a door takes under a pointer, and only the main action key and a toast are filled (Entries and rules).
- `tests/designLaw.test.mjs` holds the tags and the actions to their tiers: an `h1` is 32 or the page's name, an `h2` and an `h3` are 20 (the named exceptions excepted), and a button is main, second or link.

## Composing a screen

**The numbers make a screen correct; these make it right.** Each rule below was found the same way: a built screen was called wrong in the owner's words (quoted, so the failure is recognised when it comes back), and the screen that fixed it was approved. They bind every screen, the admin and the battle's page parts included. Before a new or changed screen is shown, check it against every rule here at 390 and 1440, in both themes, and by using it (submit the form, play the battle), not only by a still. A screen that passes `tests/designLaw.test.mjs` but breaks one of these is not done.

**What the eye meets first**

1. **Size follows use.** What is read or pressed most stands first and largest; what is read once stands smaller and later. A container is the size of what it holds: a name does not get an entry built for a picture (the battle matchup went from 84 to 64), and the party a player needs is never cut while the opponent's stands large. Failure: "커야 할 요소는 작고, 작아야 할 요소는 크고", "많이 쓰는 건 크고 잘 보이는 곳에".
2. **The subject is the hero.** A screen about a Pokémon stands the Pokémon large on its floor with its figures beside it (the records' Pokémon sheet: since 2026-11-24 one floor line across the sheet with the rank's figure at the left and the Pokémon at the right both standing on it, and the four counters under it on one shared line, so every line meets); a party is six pictures; a match is two sides facing across VS. A picture shrunk beside a list of words fails: "포켓몬도 주인공처럼 보이지 않고 그냥 이미지 하나".
3. **Show it, do not list it.** A count is a counter, a share is a bar on its track (a line of 2, filled dim, so the name and the figure beside it stay the lit things; only a slider's bar, which a finger takes hold of, and a gauge (the calculator's HP) keep 8, filled lit; 2026-11-19, the owner: "막대 디자인 ... 전부 이번 시안처럼 통일". Every bar is `Bar` in Board.jsx, those two weights and no third: it is drawn with crisp edges and its height rounded to whole device pixels, because at 125% or 150% a 2px box falls on half a pixel and every other row drew it 2 or 3 thick: "막대의 굵기가 일정하지 않은 것 같으니 ... 같은 기준으로 맞춰줘". The track is the hairline's `--rule` everywhere, the bar lit on a decided row), a type is its tag, a party is its pictures, a matchup is a ruled table of cells. Lines of label and value are the last resort, never the design. Where the data has colour (types), the colour is used, because it is content. Failure: "단순 텍스트의 나열", "너무 단조롭다", "다채로움도 없고".
4. **The fact a visitor came for is found without reading.** The deadline, the rule that decides their league, their own result stand where the eye lands: spec rows under the title, or the 439 held in view on desktop. Failure: "중요한 내용은 눈에 들어오지도 않아".

**Everything hangs from something**

5. **No word floats.** Every word, figure and picture belongs to an entry, a cell, a row or a section's rule, at that container's edge or centre. A label left in open space between two blocks is moved into the cell it names (the type boards: each type a cell of the head column, each count in its own cell, so the table has no gap). Failure: "붕 떠 있다", "우연히 텍스트가 저기 있는 느낌", "어쩌다가 저기 있는 느낌".
6. **No dead space, no crowding.** Every gap is the step of Space for the relation it shows. A band that separates nothing, two separate things touching, or one gap wider than its siblings is a bug, most often an empty element still keeping its margin (the empty console that doubled the gap above 기권). Measure it. Failure: "의미 없는 빈 공간", "너무 꽉 끼여서", "너무 붙어 있다", "다닥다닥", "공백이 다른 것들에 비해 엄청 넓어져서".
7. **Nothing touches an edge it does not belong to.** A check, a label or a picture keeps its entry's room on every width (an entry starts on the page's own edge, so what stands inside it keeps its gap to the next thing); a layout that is right on a phone is checked again on desktop. Failure: "체크박스가 너무 왼쪽에 딱 붙어 있어서".
8. **Centred is measured.** A popup, a banner, a battle plate, a word on a key is centred by its ink (The eye), never assumed from the CSS. Failure: "승리 팝업이 중앙에 정렬되지 않아".
9. **Lines meet across columns.** Two columns share their top line and their last; keys in a row of entries stand on one line (link requests two across, their keys aligned).
10. **A screen uses its window, and wastes none of it.** Desktop fills its law (the 1200, the battle included); a phone shows what is needed without scrolling past empty or oversized parts. Failure: "화면 낭비가 너무 심해", "창을 가득 채우지 않는".

**One kind, one look, everywhere**

11. **Fix the family, not the one.** When one element of a kind is wrong, every element of that kind across the site is found and fixed with it: one box with every box and popup, one type cell with every type cell, one Pokémon cell's tone with every cell, one chosen entry with every chosen entry, one way in with every way out. The owner never meets the same mistake twice. Failure: "게시판의 글 사각형만 묘하게 다른", "키키링의 배경 네모만 묘하게 색이 다르지 않아?".
12. **Same role, same size, same lines.** A pair of keys has one type size; tags in one list break alike (every type on one line); cells of one grid are one size whatever they hold. Failure: "승인과 거절 글자 크기도 다르고", "어떤 것은 타입이 두 줄로, 어떤 것은 한 줄로", "타입별로 네모 크기가 다른".
13. **The decided entry decides what is in it.** Everything inside the chosen one takes its light: its words, marks, a tag and a bar go lit with it over its 2px rule (in code `.inv` redefines `--ground` as `--lit` so nothing set in the ground's colour is lost), and nothing inside it keeps a tone of its own: a picture's cell stays transparent. No island of the old look is left inside it. Failure: "흰색으로 바뀌는데 포켓몬 배경은 남색이라 부자연스러워".
14. **Motion comes in pairs, and moments get scenes.** Whatever has an entrance has the matching exit (going back lights as going in does; a sheet closes as it opened), and no change of screen is a cut. A moment that matters (going into a battle, the leads thrown, coming to 도전의 탑) is a scene of its own: made for that moment, never another screen's scene carried over, slow enough to be read, and an entrance, never a load. Failure: "나갈 때는 창만 뚝하고 전환", "아무 애니메이션 없이 창만 변화", "두근두근함과 웅장함이 하나도 없어", "그냥 로딩되는 느낌", "엘리베이터 연출을 그대로 가져오지 말고", "너무 빨라서 뭐가 뭔지도 모르겠어".

**Nothing without a reason**

15. **Every mark answers "what is this?"** A line, a square, a band or a block that a visitor cannot name a use for goes. Leftovers count: a rule that opens nothing, a hairline showing under a sheet on a phone, an engine credit in a prominent place (the engine, music and sound credits live in one quiet line at the end of 소개, never on the battle screen; 2026-11-24, the owner: "정말 아무도 신경 안 쓰는 곳으로 치워버리든가"). Failure: "이상한 검은 줄이 있어. 무슨 용도로 만들었는지 모르겠네", "화살표 옆의 네모 세 개는 뭐야?", "뜬금 없는 자리에 뜬금 없이 존재감".
16. **Demote, do not delete.** A useful feature that is not the point (the replay keys, the whole log) moves back and down and quiets; it stays. "완전히 없애지는 마. 눈에 안 띄게만."
17. **Explain in the flow, never in a popup.** A feature is met where its need arises and is named where it stands (rule 22: by its name, never by a sentence about it): the account key leads to 내 기록 찾기 inside the records, a Google sign-in carries Google's G, the operators' login stands with the footer's other links. No explaining modal and no hidden entry. Failure: "너무 숨어 있고 안내도 없고", "눈에 너무 안 띄는데", "팝업을 띄워서 구구절절 설명하는 건 반대".
18. **A decision about someone else asks once.** Approving, rejecting and a delete that cannot be undone each confirm once; what can be undone acts at once and offers 되돌리기 (Sheets).

**Judgement, and what was approved**

19. **The best form for each element, not one rule for all.** The law gives the parts; choosing among them is judgement (planes hold, lines operate). A screen called wrong is redesigned from what people do on it, not restyled: "완전히 새롭게", "BRAND NEW".
20. **What was approved is the precedent.** Before inventing, reuse the forms that were approved after being called wrong: the one name an entry exists for at 20 with its facts as spec rows or a grey line under it; one entry for one thing; two decisions side by side at 1 to 1.618, the back one first (the link requests, the confirm dialog); fields 20 apart and sections 52; on desktop a form laid out as its notice, the form in 709 and the facts with the action in the 439 held in view; a record as counters, then bars on tracks (the Pokémon sheet); a status or a league as the type's tag; a team as six pictures on floors.
21. **Both themes are one job.** A fix made in one theme is made and checked in the other in the same change. "당연히 다크모드도 고쳐야지."

**Read without sentences**

22. **The form says it; no sentence says it again.** A visitor judges a screen by what it shows, not by lines written about it (2026-11-08, the user: "4연승이 걸린 판 과 같은 설명들은 굳이 필요 없어. 그런 텍스트 설명 없이도 사용자가 직관적으로 판단할 수 있도록 했으면 좋겠어. 이건 모든 요소에 적용될 사항이야"). State is light (done decided on its rule, live the amber mark, next lit, the rest dim or muted), a stake or a score is the number itself as a figure with its unit, a choice shows itself chosen and the key names the act it will do (바꾸고 다음 판). Words that stay are names and units: a label that names a thing (1층, 주인, 내 파티, 연승), a key, a figure's unit. A sentence stays only where nothing on the screen can say it: why a key is off or waits (운영진이 트레이너 이름을 확인하면 도전할 수 있습니다), an error and what to do about it, a confirmation's consequence. A line that restates what the screen shows, explains a rule the form already plays out, or narrates what will happen goes (연승이 걸린 판, 지면 4연승에서 도전이 끝납니다, 이긴 상대의 포켓몬 하나를 내 파티의 하나와 바꿀 수 있습니다). A rule is drawn the same way: how many may be chosen is the number of places, a kind that may come once has one place of its own, what a choice gives stands on it as chips, and a thing that cannot be chosen stands off where it is, its reason one press away. What matters more shows it by its form (the decided entry on its thick rule, a gate in orders), never by a word set over it. When something seems to need a sentence, change the form until it does not. Failure: "굳이 필요 없어", "어떤 포켓몬을 어떻게 얼마나 선택할 수 있는지 ... 기준이 무엇인지 전혀 알 수 없어", "아무런 권위도 느껴지지 않아".

**The finish**

23. **Finished, not a prototype.** A screen or a scene is judged as the shipped product of a current game or a brand's own site, never as a draft. When a flaw shows, take away: fewer parts, and what stays larger, smoother and clearer; a flaw is never covered by another layer of decoration, and something added that became a flaw goes again. Every joint gets the same finish as the whole (an arch's inside, a window against its spiral, a line's end). Failure: "파워포인트 기본 도형을 마구 덧붙여서 만든 미완성품 같아", "프로토타입 느낌", "닌텐도 64 탑 모델 같아", "뒤틀린 부분이 너무 많아", "벡터로 그린 것이 다 티날 정도로".
24. **What comes now and then has a place kept for it.** A refusal, an alert, a hint or a description lights inside a place the frame already keeps, beside its cause (a table's own head strip, the message window, the dock's toast), and moves nothing: it never widens a strip, pushes a key down or lays a box over the screen. Information opens on purpose (a press, a long press), never by a pointer resting on something. Failure: "기존의 것을 왜곡하고 비틀지 않으면서 알람을 띄우고", "타이머보다 위로 칸이 넓어지는 문제", "커서만 올려놔도 계속 설명이 뜨는 것도 문제".

**An almanac, not a spreadsheet** (2026-11-18, the owner's pick of draft B, `.design-board/2026-11-18-board-variety/`; set as the almanac 2026-11-24, `.design-board/2026-11-24-almanac/`)

25. **One form leads, and its neighbours are other forms.** A screen mixes an almanac's forms (a standing figure, a podium, a table, a list, a portrait), not one sheet of equal cells. The lead holds the one thing the screen is for and is the largest thing on it, at least 1.618 squared the height of any other block (the home: the next event and the reigning champion as one lead; the records: the podium). The wall stands people with their Pokémon side by side on one line (the past champions). Two blocks next to each other never share both a size and a form. The first three of a ranking stand as a podium over the rest (their places as thin large figures, the name, a line of their record); the rest may be a table, and a table is the one list that names its columns: small dim heads (순위, 트레이너, 우승, 준우승, 4강) over its 2px rule, figures at each row's end in Albert Sans, a zero muted. A list of equal rows that repeats one shape for no reason is still the failure. Failure: "엑셀처럼 단순한 요소가 반복돼서", "딱딱하고 진부하게".
26. **No space without a reason.** Inside an entry no band without ink is longer than 40 (measured on the rendered page at 390 and 1440, The eye). Two columns side by side end on one line because the shorter one's rows stretch, never because its column is left empty under them. Room beside a figure goes to the facts that belong to it (an event's 일시, 신청 마감 and 형식 beside its countdown; a champion's six under the champion), and a grid fills its last row with real entries or ends a row early, never with an empty cell. Failure: "무의미한 공백이나 여백", "전혀 그럴 필요가 없는데 남아있는 빈 공간".

**Judged, not only measured** (2026-11-23: a whole-site audit found the numbers kept everywhere and the screens still reading as machine-made, because rules were carried out to the letter where the content asked for less; the owner: "기계적으로 체계를 지키고 있기 때문이 아닐까")

27. **A control is as wide as what it holds.** On desktop a search field may take the room that is left, but a select, a row of chips or a key is as wide as its words (a select 220, a key its word and its padding, 44 or 52 high), and it stands beside what it acts on, never across the 1200 from it. The 1200 belongs to lists, tables and entries. A select of one short word stretched across the page, or a key 760 from the field it submits, is a phone layout stretched. Reading (a post, a note under a table) stays inside the 709.
28. **Actions are ranked on the screen as they are in use.** One main action (52, filled lit) a screen. What is used next stands as second actions (52, outlined). What is used now and then (a share, a calendar file, a rare cancel) stands smaller: a 44 key in a row, or an underlined word. Keys of different rank never stack as equals, and the rarest act is never the widest key.
29. **Nothing is stretched to make a line meet.** Two columns end on one line when their content lets them. An entry is never lengthened into a band without ink, a grid is never padded with empty places, and a row is never widened so that its end reaches an edge: the shorter thing ends short. (This is rule 26 read strictly; a medal board's columns follow the number of its medals.)
30. **The hairline joins one kind.** Entries of one kind (rows of a list, cells of a table, keys of one row) follow one another with only their hairline or 2 between them. Two different kinds of thing (a row of chips and a select, a list and the key under it) stand 12 apart or more.

## Made by people

## Figures

**The page announces in figures, the line reads in type.** A figure is a number set large in Albert Sans with tabular digits (`Fig` in `src/components/common/Fig.jsx`, the class `.fig`): days left, a start time and a clock, a score, a rank on a podium, a champion's generation, a live counter, the calculator's damage range and speed, 404. It replaces the seven-segment display everywhere (2026-11-24).

- **The larger the thinner.** 200 from 84 up, 300 from 40, 400 under 40. Its size is the height of its digits; the font size is that height over .72.
- **A leading zero is dropped** (5, not 05), except in a clock (13:00). Nothing to show is a muted dash.
- **Whole numbers only.** A figure never carries a decimal; the calculator shows 68 ~ 81 as figures and 68.4% ~ 81.0% in type under it.
- **Its unit is type,** 20 or 15 (32 beside a hero), on the figure's baseline: 5 일, 6 대.
- **Never under 32.** Below that it is type.
- A count of the site's own things is not shown at all (Made by people). Everything read in a line (a date, a rank in a table, points in a list) is Albert Sans with tabular digits at its tier's size.

## Entries and rules

1. **Nothing stands on a surface.** `--panel` and `--panel-2` are the ground itself; no radius, no border box, no shadow anywhere. `--tint` is the one step a door takes under a pointer.
   Nothing is ringed in a colour either: a thing that carries a colour (a type's move key, the red 기권, a tower door in its hue) is its colour alone, and when it is chosen the rule goes under it, never round it (2026-11-24, the owner: "이런 색이 있는 건 외곽선이 있으니 부자연스러워"). In the battle and the tower a card or a key is an entry closed by the hairline under it, and a plate over the picture is one solid colour with no edge.
2. **An entry is closed by a hairline.** What was a panel (`.pn`, `.pn2`) is an entry with a 1px `--rule` under it; a list is a stack of entries. No other line is drawn inside an entry.
3. **A section opens with a thick rule.** A table, a podium, a list or a contents block opens with a 2px `--lit` rule. A table's small dim column heads (순위, 트레이너, 우승), the one place a list names its columns, stand in a 44 band under the rule, closed by a hairline: the rule runs across the whole table with nothing over it, so it parts what stands above (a podium) from the table (2026-11-24, the owner: under the heads it read as an underline of the small words, shorter than the rows).
4. **An entry is a door.** Pressing it goes where its fact lives; one that leads to an address is a link to it.
5. **Rows have fixed heights.** 52 for one line, 64 for a title and a grey line, 44 for a key and value inside a spec, 32 for a match's head strip.
6. **The record holder is decided** (Emphasis): set larger, and over the thick rule.
7. **Golden splits.** A page divided in two divides 1.618 to 1, never in half unless the two are equal.
8. **Controls are lines and solid squares, never outlines** (2026-11-24, the owner: "외곽선이 있는 네모칸이 전부 너무너무 어색해", and a box whose outline fills when pressed, as the comment box did, likewise). No control, key, menu or square is ringed by a `box-shadow` or a border, at rest, under a pointer, in focus or chosen; the one ring left is a keyboard's `:focus-visible` on a key or a link (`tests/designLaw.test.mjs`). A field is its value over a 1px `--dim` line that thickens to 2px `--lit` in focus (by a click as by a key, never a ring) or in error; a long text is a solid `--tint` block, focus laying the 2px line along its bottom; a check and a switch are solid squares (Controls); a second action is a word key; a menu is a solid `--tint` plane; tabs, chips and choices are words with the chosen one over the 2px rule; a sheet is the ground itself over the veil, one solid colour with no edge of another tone (2026-11-23, the owner: 전부 단색으로).
9. **A control row stands on one line.** A search, its chips and a select that act on one list stand 52 tall on ONE 1px `--dim` line drawn under the whole row (`.ctl-row` in board.css); a part never keeps a line of its own at another height. A chosen chip's rule, a focused search's line and an open select's line fall on that same line. On a phone the search takes its own line and the rest share the next; from 860 everything stands on one line (2026-11-24, the owner: three lines at three heights read "분절된 것처럼").

Every row of one list takes the same inset; a section's head stays on the column's edge. A cell that opens a select keeps its words `--band` inside it; a key keeps its words 20 inside it.
11. **A control that a list follows opens it.** A search, a control row or the tools over a list, when the list stands straight under them, draw the list's 2px `--lit` rule as their own line, and the list draws no rule and keeps no gap of its own: one line, never the control's line and the list's rule one on the other (2026-11-24, the owner: "두 줄이 있어 보이는 경우"). ## Typesetting

**The type is set, not poured.** Type gives the sizes; this chapter is how a line of them is set: the face, where a line breaks, how long it runs, how far apart lines stand, which edge it hangs from, and what never parts. Each rule holds on every screen, the admin and the battle's page parts included, in both themes. Most are written once in `tokens.css`'s base and held by `tests/designLaw.test.mjs`; the rest are measured on the page (The eye).

**The faces**

- **Two faces and no third.** `--font` sets Albert Sans first, so the Latin letters and every digit in a Korean line are Albert Sans and the Hangul falls through to Freesentation; the system fonts after them only stand in until a slice arrives. No serif, no monospace, no display face, no handwriting, no web font from a CDN.
- **Upright only.** Nothing is italic or oblique: Hangul has no italic, and a slanted Hangul is a machine's. `i` and `em` are upright, and a word is stressed by light or weight 700, never by a slant, an underline that is not a link, or quotation marks.
- **Nothing is faked.** `font-synthesis: none`: a weight or a style the files do not have is never drawn by the browser, so a missing 700 shows as a missing file, never as a smeared bold.
- **Three weights for words, each with a job.** 400 reads (a paragraph, a grey line), 600 labels and operates (a label, a field, a tab, a chip), 700 names (a title, a name, a key's word). A figure takes its own weight by its size (200 from 84, 300 from 40, 400 under; Figures), from Albert Sans's own files. A line never mixes weights to decorate; a 700 inside a 400 sentence is a figure or a name, never a phrase for stress.
- **Every digit is tabular.** Figures in a column line up by place, a clock that changes does not jitter, and a score never shifts its neighbours. Albert Sans has no `tnum` feature, so the file itself is built with tabular digits (2026-11-19, the owner's pick A): `scripts/build-albert-sans.py` gives all ten the zero's width at every weight, each centred on it, and leaves every other glyph as drawn. `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on the body stays as the statement of the rule.
- **Tracking tightens with size and never opens.** 0 at 12 and 15, -.01em at 20, -.02em at 32 and the page name, -.04em on a figure (`--ls-fig`). Korean is never tracked open: spaced-out letters are a slogan's, not an almanac's.

**Leading: the more a text may run, the more room between its lines**

| Text | Size / line | Ratio | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| a label, a grey line | 12/16 | 1.33 | one line, read in a glance |
| a row title, a key, a field | 15/20 | 1.33 | one line inside a box whose height is set |
| reading, and anything that may run to a third line | 15/24 | 1.6 | Hangul is dense and square; the eye needs the open line to find the next one |
| a section title | 20/28 | 1.4 | at most two lines |
| a thing's title | 32/40 | 1.25 | large type holds itself together |
| a page name | 52/60, 84/92 | 1.15, 1.1 | one line; the larger the tighter |
| a figure | its digits' height | about .72 of its size | a figure is one line; `Fig` sets it |

- A line height is a token, never `normal` (it differs between the two faces, so a line would jump when a slice loads) and never a bare ratio. `1` only on a box whose height its container sets (a digit in a counter, a figure in a chip).
- Text that can wrap to a third line is reading and takes 15/24, whatever its weight.

**Measure: how long a line of reading runs**

- **A reading line holds about 20 to 47 syllables.** On a phone that is the column (about 22 at 390); on desktop it is the 709, about 47 at 15. Reading never runs across the 1200: at about 80 syllables a line the eye loses its way back to the next one. A page of one kind that holds reading (a policy) keeps its reading in the 709; its 439 holds what belongs beside it, never nothing (Composing a screen 26). A notice and a board post are the owner's exception (2026-11-24: the 709 and a 439 held in view beside it read as shut in, "의미 없이 갇혀 있는 느낌"): they read across the 1200 in one column.
- The measure comes from the column, never from a width in characters (`ch`, `em`) on the text itself (Type).
- A short line (a label, a row's title, a grey line) has no widest measure; it runs to its cell.

**Where a line breaks**

- **Korean breaks between words, never inside one** (`word-break: keep-all` on the body). `word-break: break-all` and `break-word` are never used: they split 트레이너 into 트레이 and 너.
- **A run with no space** (an address, a long English name, a code someone typed) may break anywhere (`overflow-wrap: anywhere`), and only inside what people write: a notice, a post, a comment, a form's answers.
- **No hyphens and no hand breaks.** A break is never forced with `<br>` or a space run in the site's own words (Type).
- **The last line is never one word alone.** A paragraph is `text-wrap: pretty`; a title is `balance`, so its two lines stand near one length.
- **What never parts** stands as one unit (`white-space: nowrap` on its own box), and when a row is short of room the free words around it give way first and end in an ellipsis: a figure and its unit (32명, 6대, 5 일), a date with its weekday (11월 7일 (토)), a time (오후 3:05), a range (68 ~ 81), a score (2 : 1), a trainer's name, a Pokémon's name, a key's or a tab's word, a tag, a chip. A key's word is one line always: a key too narrow for its word gets a shorter word, never two lines.
- **How long a thing may run** is its tier's: a page title two lines, a section title two lines, a row's title one line, each then an ellipsis; reading has no limit; a label one line.

**Which edge a line hangs from**

- **Words hang from the start edge, ragged at the end.** Never justified: justified Hangul stretches the gaps between words into rivers.
- **Centred is for one line that stands alone in a box of its own:** a key's or a choice's word, a grid cell's name (a title medal, a type cell), a figure in a fixed cell, a figure's caption on a page led by its figure. A paragraph, a list of lines, a page title over a stack and a section's head are never centred: a column of centred lines is the first look of a machine-made page.
- **Figures in a column hang from the end edge** on their tabular digits, so places line up; a rank number stands centred in its fixed cell.
- **Two lines that belong together share one start edge** by their ink, a title and its grey line, a label and its value (The eye).

**Space inside a line**

- One space between words, never two. A figure and its unit join with none (32명, 6대, 15%). Hangul beside Latin or digits takes a space only where Korean grammar puts one (YPL 시즌 3, 39회 파이컵).
- Paragraphs part by 32 and a heading inside reading stands 20 over its paragraph (`.prose`); a paragraph is never indented and never parted by an empty line of `<br>`.
- A mark stands before its word in one unit (→ 파티 보기), and the pair never parts (What never parts).

## When the law is silent

**Nothing new is an exception.** The law is meant to answer every case; when a new part seems to fall outside it, the answer is found in this order, and the first step that answers ends the search.

1. **The same role already stands somewhere.** Use that form exactly: its tier, its tokens, its component in `Board.jsx`, its motion. A list of things is a stack of entries, a figure someone asks for is a counter or a figure in type, a pick is a choice, a filter is a chip, a refusal is a sentence in a kept place (Composing a screen 24).
2. **The nearest role by meaning.** A new thing takes the tier, the line and the light of what it most resembles in use, never in look: a thing pressed is a key of one of the three actions, a thing read once is a label, a thing that changes live is a figure with the amber mark.
3. **Fewer parts.** If two forms seem to fit, the one with fewer parts and fewer words wins (Composing a screen 23).
4. **Only then a change to the law.** A new value, a new form or a new kind of motion is written here first, with the owner's word and the reason it could not be one of the above, then in `tokens.css` and the shared parts, then on the screen. A value is never invented in place, a component never carries a private copy of a shared part, and `EXCEPT` in `tests/designLaw.test.mjs` names only a reason written in this file.

A screen built this way cannot be told from one the owner approved, which is the test: put it beside its neighbours, and nothing about it says it came later.

## Motion

**A page is set; it does not fly.** Four verbs, all transform and opacity on a picture already drawn. The battle stage moves by its own rules (Battle) and is the only place outside them. The site is meant to feel finished, not still: what arrives rises the last 12 into its place as it lights, and everything a pointer can press answers it.

## Voice and tone

Words are under the law too. Korean, plain and factual: what happens, then what to do. These rules cover the words the site itself writes, never what people write in it, and never the game's own battle messages: those are quoted as the game prints them (Showdown's Korean text, taken from Pokémon Champions), 반말 and exclamation marks included, and only the site's words around them follow this chapter. The message window says only what the game says (decided 2026-11-07): a line the engine marks `[silent]` (Yawn wearing off before the sleep, Regenerator, the items Trick trades), a note Showdown writes in brackets (the weather going on, Roost) and an ability's "[…의 …]" line (the ability plate shows it) are never printed, and each line is picked and filled the way Showdown's own client does, so no name is ever left blank.

**Register.** Polite formal 합니다체, one voice everywhere.

| Where | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Why a key is off or waits (the one place guidance stays, Composing a screen 22) | ~합니다 | 운영진이 트레이너 이름을 확인하면 도전할 수 있습니다. |
| Asking someone to act | ~해 주세요 | 제목을 입력해 주세요. |
| Confirming an action | ~할까요? | 이 공지를 삭제할까요? |
| Something finished | ~했습니다 | 저장했습니다. |
| Errors | ~하지 못했습니다, then a request | 글을 올리지 못했습니다. 잠시 뒤 다시 시도해 주세요. |
| Buttons, links, tabs | Noun form | 저장, 대진표 보기 |
| Status labels | Short noun phrase | 진행 중, 종료, 대기 |
| Field hints | 예: and a real value | 예: 37회 파이컵 |

**Labels.** One form: a noun. Sino-Korean verbs lose 하기 (저장, 신청, 등록, 수정); native verbs keep 기 (보기, 만들기, 올리기, 내려받기). Object first (대진표 만들기). One action has one name on every screen. No `+` before a label in text, no 하세요 on a button.

**Particles.** A name takes the particle its last sound asks for (가디안은, 리자몽을, 한카리아스가; `src/services/josa.js`), never both in brackets (은(는), 을(를)): a bracketed pair reads as written by a machine (2026-11-10: the builder's rule errors and the record apply's note).

**Punctuation.** No exclamation marks. A sentence ends with a period wherever it stands; a label takes none. Ellipsis is `…`, only on visible progress text and a field prompt. Quotes are curly. **No middle dot**: a list takes commas. No em dash in a sentence. A range in prose takes an en dash; the calculator's damage range is the one tilde, because it is a figure, not prose. Arrows only beside a stat (S↑ C↓; in a battle a stage as the games show it, 공격 ▲2) and as the → of an action.

**Numbers, dates, time.** Dates through `formatDate` (11월 7일 (토)); in a tight row digits and dots (10.28) with the weekday dim beside them; time 오후 3:05 in reading text and 13:00 as a clock figure. Number and unit with no space (32명, 6대).

**English.** Names only. YPL, Yonsei Pokémon League, Pokémon with its accent. 디스코드 in running Korean. No all-caps labels. No emoji.

**Errors.** What did not happen, then what to do. Never a bare noun (저장 실패), never an error code or a technical word for a visitor.

**Page titles.** What one looks at, an en dash, the brand: `칭호 – YPL`, `38회 파이컵 공지 – YPL`; the home is `YPL – 포켓몬 센터 연세점 배틀 리그` (Layout and pages, The tab's title); a hidden battle tab whose choice is awaited reads `선택할 차례 – …`.

**Official names.** A thing is called by its official name: 포켓몬 챔피언스, never 포챔스 or another short form; YPL and Yonsei Pokémon League as written. A share preview and a tab title say the name and nothing else.

**No filler.** A line earns its place by saying something the screen does not already show. What reads as written by a machine goes: a sentence that repeats its label or its heading, reassurance (걱정하지 마세요, 간편하게, 손쉽게), a summary of what was just seen, a list of benefits, a line that addresses the reader's feelings. When unsure, the shorter sentence that states the fact wins.

**Don't.** Claims with no fact behind them, pushing, invitations to a feeling, and 당신.

## Accessibility

- **AA** in both themes for all text; the amber mark at 3 or more.
- **Never light alone.** The amber mark stands beside a word; a winner reads by its weight, its rule and the word 승자 or the result; an error by the `!` square and a sentence; a heart by its fill and the words 남은 하트 N개 read out.
- **Touch:** 44 by 44 at least on a phone; rows and fields are 52; in a battle everything pressed every turn is 52 or more (the moves 64). A phone is a finger and a keyboard (2026-11-10): two quick taps never zoom the page (`touch-action: manipulation` on the root; a pinch still zooms); what a focus or a keyboard brings into view stops clear of the fixed bars, the tab bar and a docked action included (`scroll-padding` on the root); on iOS a field's focus does not zoom the page (the fields are 15, under Safari's 16, so `main.jsx` adds `maximum-scale=1` there only); a battle's whole phone screen takes no text selection or callout; the sound wakes when a finger lifts, not only when it lands; and a phone on its side keeps the battle's head and deck clear of the notch.
- **Keyboard:** everything by key, in reading order, with the one focus ring; a battle and a replay also answer their game keys (Battle).
- **Long press** shows what a thing is; it never does anything, so a press held too long only opens information. Sheets lock what is behind them with `inert`. The first stop is 본문으로 건너뛰기.
- **A figure is read as its value.** Each `Fig` is an image role carrying its value with its unit as text for assistive technology (`aria-label="5일"`); an empty one's dash is not read as a number.
- **Reduced motion:** see Motion.

## Don't

- Do not give a thing a surface: no panel, card, frame, radius or shadow. An entry is closed by a hairline and a section opens with the 2px rule; only the main action and a toast are filled.
- Do not add a hue. Amber is the one mark; status reads through light and words.
- Do not draw a number as a segment display, a dial or a pip row; do not set a reading number as a figure, a figure under 32, or a decimal in a figure.
- Do not give a Pokémon a box of its own or let it float: it stands on its line.
- Do not blur, glow, gradient, sheen or grain anything.
- Do not move what stands: no sliding pages, no rolling or counting digits, no bouncing cards. What arrives may rise 12 as it lights, a pointer may nudge a picture or an arrow 4, and the battle stage moves by its own rules (Battle); nothing else travels.
- Do not animate a shadow, filter, clip, mask or background; move transform and opacity on a picture already drawn.
- Do not use View Transitions snapshots or copy the page for a transition.
- Do not put a small label above a title, or repeat one fact twice on a screen.
- Do not slant, fake a weight, justify, hyphenate, track open, or break a Korean word; do not centre a paragraph or run reading across the 1200 (Typesetting).
- Do not invent a value, a form or an exception in place: find it by When the law is silent, or change the law first.
- Do not change data behaviour from a visual change.
